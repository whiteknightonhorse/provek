RED-028 - the gate written to catch an unpinned pip line could be walked past four ways
====================================================================================================

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS. `RED-027` shows `LAW-CI-PIP-HASH-PINNED` catching the edit it was built for:
a `pip install` line reverted to bare packages. Fable then refuted the gate itself, on the ground
that a control is judged by what gets PAST it, and found two ways in - a trailing shell comment and
a block-scalar header the hand-written reader did not recognise. Both were green. Both are in the
core of the guarantee rather than at its edge, and the first is reachable by an ordinary edit:
a developer comments out the tail of a command while debugging and does not put it back.

The four cases below are the two findings plus the two neighbours they implied - the `echo` variant
of the comment bypass, which is the printed excuse containing the string being matched, and the
anchor spelling of the block header.

WHAT WAS DONE. Both readings were run against the SAME fixtures: the gate as it stood when Fable
refuted it, taken from the git index, and the repaired gate. Nothing was reverted in the tree; the
before-copy was loaded from `git show :scripts/verify_pip_pins.py` into a separate module, so the
working file was never edited and its sha256 was verified unchanged either side of the run.

READING IT. `installs seen=0` in the two block-scalar cases is the worse of the two failures and the
reason neither was caught by anything else here. The reader did not merely misjudge the line - it
never saw it, and reported clean over an empty measurement. The vacuity guard in `check()` exists
for exactly that and could not fire, because the three genuine inline installs in `gates.yml` keep
the count above zero. A gate blinded WITHOUT a signal is indistinguishable from a gate that looked
and found nothing, which is invariant 1 turned on the instrument.

THE REPAIR. `strip_comment` cuts at an unquoted `#`, tracking quotes so that a `#` inside them stays
data; `segments` splits a line into the commands a shell would actually run, so a later `echo`
cannot vouch for an earlier install; `BLOCK_HEADER` recognises the indentation and chomping
indicators and an anchor or tag in front of them. Quoted text is KEPT, unlike the door's reader,
because `pip install "--require-hashes"` really does pass the flag - removing it would invent a
false red instead of catching a true one.

WHAT IS STILL NOT CLAIMED. This reads the TEXT of `run:` steps, not what they execute. A step that
runs a shell script which installs packages from inside is invisible here; no such indirection
exists in this tree, and the limit is written in the function's own docstring rather than left for
the next reader to discover. Found by Fable, third finding of the same round.

AND THE REPAIR ABOVE WAS ITSELF REFUTED - read `RED-029` next. Three more ways past were found in
the round that reviewed this one, two of them introduced BY the repair recorded here. The four
cases below are closed instances, not a closed class, and this paragraph is the correction of a
closing sentence that once implied otherwise.
